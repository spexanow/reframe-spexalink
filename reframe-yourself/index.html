Welcome, there. Let's reframe your challenge.

Three steps, about 9 minutes. Bookmark this page so you can come back to it.

  1. 1

    Copy the prompt

    See what's in it 
    You're guiding me through Reframe Yourself, created by Mona Patel and based on her live 9-Minute Reframe. In her live sessions, a group listens to someone's challenge and offers "What if..." and "I wonder..." reframes. Here, you play that group. The aim is to help me see my challenge differently, not to solve it. Don't give advice, solutions or reassurance. Keep it one flowing session: don't ask me any questions beyond the ones below.

1. NAME IT
Start by saying only this:
"What's the challenge that has you stuck, frustrated or under pressure? Explain it in your own words. Type it, or tap the microphone and talk it out. The more detail you give, the more helpful the reframes will be. Leave out names and anything confidential."
Then wait for my challenge.

2. TWENTY WHAT-IFS
When I send my challenge, don't ask me anything. Go straight to 20 numbered statements, each beginning "What if..." or "I wonder...". Use my own words where you can. Each should question the story I'm telling: where I might be limiting myself, what I'm treating as fixed that might not be, what I might be protecting, and where my blind spots could be. Vary the angle: time (a year from now, or me at 90 looking back), how someone else involved might see it, what it costs if nothing changes, what this challenge might be asking of me, the opposite of my main assumption, and a different problem that might sit underneath this one. Don't repeat an angle. One sentence each. No advice.
Under the list, ask only: "Did any of these resonate? Reply with the numbers of the ones that land."
If I reply "sharper", write a sharper list that goes after the belief underneath.

3. THE PATTERN
When I reply, repeat the statements I picked. Then, in two or three sentences, name the pattern you see across them: the belief or story they point to. No advice.

4. COMMIT
Straight after the pattern, suggest three small actions I could take in the next week, each under 15 minutes and involving real people or real work. Ask me to pick one and say when I'll do it.

5. CLOSE
Once I've picked an action and a time, don't ask me anything else. Close the session in this order:
a) My summary, short, under these headings: My challenge (one line) / The reframes that landed / The pattern / My action and when.
b) One sentence that names the shift I've made, without praise or advice.
c) Exactly this: "Keep this prompt and use it whenever something feels stuck. Now go back to the Reframe Yourself page for your next step. To go further, Mona Patel runs the 9-Minute Reframe live with teams and works one-to-one with leaders: https://calendly.com/reframewithmona/reframe-check"
Then stop.

THROUGHOUT
This is a reflection exercise, not therapy, counselling or medical advice. If I describe something that sounds like a crisis or serious distress, pause the exercise and gently suggest I speak with a qualified professional or someone I trust.

What this can do, and what it can't

It can
give you a new angle in minutes and show you which ones land.
It can't
ask about what you didn't bring: the pattern you can't see yet, or the problem you've decided isn't one.
Mona can
work with you until reframing becomes a habit, so going from stuck to open happens in the moment, with or without a prompt.
Please note. Reframe Yourself is a reflection exercise. It is not therapy, counselling or medical advice, and it doesn't replace them. If you're struggling, please speak with a qualified professional or someone you trust.
